Draw one closed loop through the circles: turn on every black pearl, run
straight through every white one.

## What it is

A grid scattered with white and black circles. A single closed loop must pass
through every circle (and may pass through empty cells), moving between cell
centres horizontally and vertically, never crossing itself. At a **white**
circle the loop goes straight through but must turn in at least one of the two
neighbouring cells. At a **black** circle the loop turns 90° and must run
straight for at least one cell on both sides of the turn.

## How to play

Black circles near an edge are the entry point: a black pearl on the border
has only one way to satisfy "straight on both sides". Chain the local rules —
a white pearl on the edge runs along it; two adjacent black pearls force their
turns apart. The single-loop rule finishes the job: any move that would close
a small loop early is forbidden.

Big boards climb a ladder of techniques:

- **Basics:** a white pearl goes straight through, so if one side is blocked
  it runs the other way; a black pearl turns and runs two cells straight on
  both sides, so a leg that would hit the border, a cross, another black
  pearl or a sideways line is impossible and the opposite leg is drawn. A
  cell the loop passes through has exactly two lines.
- **Patterns:** a white pearl must turn in a cell next to it — so three whites
  in a row cannot run along that row, and once one neighbour is forced
  straight the other must turn.
- **No small loops:** never join the two ends of a path unless that finishes
  the whole puzzle.
- **Inside and outside:** the squares between cell centres are inside or
  outside the loop; a line separates them, a cross joins them, and chains of
  these settle edges far apart.
- **What if (Hard):** imagine an edge drawn (or crossed), follow the easier
  rules, and if something breaks, it must be the other way.

## Purpose

The most elegant of the loop puzzles — two rules, no numbers, and boards that
read as ornament. It contributes the workspace's Hamiltonian-cycle machinery:
the answer loop is built by merging 2×2 block cycles along a spanning tree,
which is also how the kolam crate thinks about its weave.

## History

Nikoli, 2000. First published as *Shiroshinju Kuroshinju* ("white pearls and
black pearls"); the name **Masyu** ("evil influence") arose when Nikoli's
president misread the kanji for *shinju* (pearl) — and the misreading stuck.

## This implementation

- **Spec knobs:** `rows`, `cols` (4–10 even on the count path; a side
  above 10 takes the ladder path, 4–24 even), `difficulty`, `cell`, `line`.
- **Generation:** answer first — a Hamiltonian cycle is constructed by merging
  block four-cycles along a random spanning tree, circles are read off the
  loop's own geometry (turns flanked by straights become black, straights
  flanked by turns become white), then circles are thinned until each one is
  load-bearing.
- **Guarantees:** `count_loops(2) == 1` over the printed circles, and every
  circle re-checked against the loop it was read from.
- **Difficulty:** circle density, honestly labelled as a proxy
  (`rating_basis`), with raw `search_nodes` reported beside it — an
  effort-based rating was tried, cost 15×, and did not normalise across board
  sizes, so it was reverted and the attempt recorded.
- **Version 1.1 — large boards on a technique ladder:** a board with a side
  above 10 (4–24 per side, rounded down to even) is generated by `lako-loop`.
  The loop is the same Hamiltonian construction, stirred by random square
  flips that keep one loop through every cell; loops whose full circle set
  the ladder cannot settle (a patch a second loop could cross) are redrawn.
  Circles are then removed only while the sound `lako-loop` masyu ladder still
  settles every edge from the circles alone — the uniqueness proof at these
  sizes. The band is the **hardest rung** the solve needed
  (`rating_basis: "hardest_rung"`, `hardest_rung`, `rung_uses`): basics →
  Kids, patterns → Easy, no small loops and inside/outside → Medium,
  what-if → Hard, both ways / deep what-if → Expert. Medium and Hard are
  met at every size; Kids and Easy at 12–16 on some seeds and otherwise
  served as Medium (big boards need the loop rule even with every circle
  printed); Expert is served as Hard unless a loop's own circles need the
  deep rungs — a deep thinning pass never found a circle only it could
  remove. Odd sides were tried (a detour strip through the extra row) and
  every such board up to 21×20 was ambiguous, so sides stay even. The tests
  run the ladder path at 8×8 and re-prove it with the loop count, and
  `lako-loop` checks every rung against brute force on small boards. Boards
  up to 10×10 are byte-identical to 1.0 (count proof, density rating).
